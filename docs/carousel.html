NIDHI PARKER01
The problem

A model on its own
generates from what
it learned in training.

It doesn't inherently give you source-level provenance, and its knowledge has a cutoff.

On regulation, that gap matters. An answer you can't trace back to the source is hard to trust.

THE PROBLEM02
What RAG changes

Retrieve first.
Then answer only
from what came back.

Built over four real-world governance sources:

  • NIST AI Risk Management Framework RISK GUIDANCE
  • NIST Generative AI Profile GENAI RISK

THE FINDING05
Why more didn't work

Some answers required following a trail. One section points at another, and neither means anything alone.

Annex IIIlists CV screening as high-risk
→
Article 6defines the conditions

111 of the 403 sections cite another section like this. In retrieval terms: multi-hop.

MULTI-HOP06
Following the citations instead

Cross-reference questions answered

7/8

Up from 3/8. The system records every cross-reference when it parses the documents, then follows them at query time.

No agent. No second model call. It can't invent a link that isn't in the text.

THE FIX07
Across the full evaluation
24/26retrieved the evidence needed
4/4unsupported questions correctly refused
0answers returned without citations

Refusal is enforced where the answer is written, not by a confidence score. The highest-scoring unanswerable question outscored the lowest-scoring answerable one.

RESULTS08
One evaluation set is never enough

answer in 45.

Graded by a separate model against the source material, blind to how the system produced the answers.

When retrieval fell short, it declined nine times for every answer it got wrong.

HELD OUT09
What I'd take from it

RAG isn't “put
documents in a model
and ask questions.”

It's the source data. The retrieval logic. The failure modes.

And whether your evaluation is actually testing the system, or quietly validating the choices you already made.
